Case study · Search & demand decomposition

Everyone blamed rankings. Rankings were one percent of it.

Organic traffic fell by nearly a quarter in a year. I decomposed the loss into seven measured causes that sum exactly to the observed decline — and the cause everyone was organized around turned out to be the smallest one.

When2026 · ~10 weeks
RoleSole analyst, method & delivery
DataSearch console · 5,000-keyword rank panel · session analytics · auction data
MethodsIndex (LMDI) decomposition · difference-in-differences · falsification tests

The situation

At a national omnichannel retailer, organic search clicks on the brand's own name had fallen by nearly half year-over-year — while reported average position improved. Organic search was roughly a third of site traffic but roughly two-thirds of attributed ecommerce revenue, so this was not an SEO-team problem; it was a revenue problem with an eight-figure annual exposure.

Two narratives were already circulating: “SEO is failing” and “AI is eating search.” Each implied a different — expensive — response. My brief, in effect: stop the storytelling and size the causes.

What I did

I treated sessions as ground truth and every third-party source — search console, the rank panel, ads auction data — as directional evidence against it. The decline was split into brand and non-brand on disjoint query populations, decomposed separately, and forced to reconcile: the two halves had to sum back to the total, with no unexplained remainder. Anything that couldn’t be measured got labeled as such rather than absorbed into a convenient bucket.

The causal factors — rank movement, paid crowding, SERP modules, demand, AI Overviews — entered as index decompositions rather than regressions, for a specific reason: a regression’s intercept silently absorbs anything that hits every keyword at once, which is exactly what a SERP-layout change does. Same data, same variable, the regression saw a fraction of what the index accounted for.

Then I tried to kill my own answer. A named algorithm update was refuted on timing (our break predates it by four weeks). A difference-in-differences result was computed, found confounded, and withdrawn. The residual-allocation choice was settled by testing against an independent third-party zero-click series rather than by preference.

The turn

The brand’s head term held the #1 blue-link position for over a year of consecutive periods while its clicks fell by nearly half. On desktop — a clean control with no map pack and where AI Overviews never rendered — clicks still fell ~60% across two years at uninterrupted rank #1. Position wasn’t lost; the click was intercepted before it reached the blue links.

The AI narrative fared no better: AI Overviews never rendered on the head brand term in either year, AI-assistant referrals were a rounding error of brand organic, and industry panel data showed click-through falling almost as hard on queries with no AI Overview at all. And paid search wasn’t cannibalizing organic — ad depth above the organic results was flat, and when paid spend was actually cut, the two organic series didn’t flinch.

The question changed from “how do we fix our rankings?” to “which surfaces still return a click — and are we the best result on those?”

What happened after

Two earlier dashboards built on the wrong cut of the data were retired and replaced with a by-surface view that leadership still uses. The exec Q&A shipped with every claim labeled measured / inferred / not established — that labeling, more than any single number, is what let the answer survive the room.

Three decisions moved: rank tracking was demoted from the headline KPI (it no longer predicts traffic; click-through is tracked in its own right), recovery investment was pointed at local and store surfaces, the shopping feed, and owned demand rather than at chasing AI-search optimization, and the revenue exposure was booked as gross — not incremental — which kept an inflated recovery case out of the plan.

Technical notes, for the people who will ask

Decomposition. Log-mean Divisia (LMDI-I) with base-year Laspeyres weights, decomposing clicks — never impression-weighted CTR, which double-counts mix. Causes are constrained to all-negative slices of the loss pool, each capped at its own surface’s size, summing exactly to the observed decline.

Why not regression. A per-period cross-sectional regression identifies factors from between-keyword variation; a SERP-layout change moves all keywords together and lands in the intercept. On the same variable, the index attribution recovered roughly the full effect where the regression recovered 10–40%.

Bias handling. Survivor-bias adjustment on AI Overview exposure (the panel only observes surviving queries), a median filter on module-presence flags to remove flicker, and deliberate haircuts on rank and module bands documented in the methodology rather than buried.

Falsification. Timing tests against named algorithm updates; a DiD across ~5,000 keywords on a rank-flip event, computed and withdrawn when confounded; residual allocation validated against an independent zero-click series (the alternative overshot it 1.6× and halved shape correlation).

Stack. Python; search console + enterprise rank panel + session analytics + ads auction data, cross-validated; ~160 commits over ten weeks.
