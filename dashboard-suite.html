Case study · BI engineering & decision support

The BI stack is a folder of HTML files.

Fifteen-plus dashboards and the Wednesday leadership deck, generated by Python, published as plain HTML files, refreshed by 36 scheduled jobs, footed to the dollar. No BI licenses. No server. No queue.

When2026 · ongoing
RoleSole builder · design system author
StackPython · BigQuery · vanilla JS · static hosting
Scale15+ dashboards · ~36 scheduled refresh jobs · 11-module weekly deck

The problem

Leadership wanted exec-ready views across marketing, merchandising, and product. Enterprise BI meant licenses, a platform queue, and charts that never matched the deck. Notebooks meant nothing a VP could open on a phone at 7 a.m. And every one of these views ends up screenshotted into a deck or a Slack thread, where a sloppy table reads as a wrong number even when it's right.

The build

  • Each dashboard is a Python generator that emits one self-contained HTML file. Data embedded, all interactivity client-side. Publishing is copying a file. No BI licenses, no server.
  • A generated hub with a staleness dot per tile, refreshed by 36 scheduled jobs.
  • A written design standard. Value and delta columns always adjacent. Rate changes in basis points, colored by direction-of-good. One blue for the focus period, one orange for the baseline, everywhere.
  • A footing rule. Money rows must match the attribution source of truth to the dollar. People add reports together; a bridge that reads lower is dead on arrival.
  • The flagship: a paid-search funnel decomposition that splits any revenue change into eight additive effects summing exactly to the total, so a spend shift into low-converting tactics cannot masquerade as a broken funnel. Replica below: real interface, synthetic data.

What it showed

The week it paid for itself: brand paid-search clicks fell 25%. The room's reflex was "we lost position to a competitor, raise bids." The auction panel said 96% impression share and 0% lost to budget, in both years. The decline was not buyable. Eligible searches were down and click-through was eroding. A six-figure "allocation loss" was reclassified from a budget lever to a demand signal, and the bid increase never happened.

The most useful thing a dashboard did all year wasn't showing a number. It stopped a confident wrong action while there was still time.

It polices itself, too. One week a view mixed a complete seven-day total with a six-day slice from a stale cache, and a funnel rate dropped about 1,000 basis points across all four tactics at once. Real click-quality shifts are tactic-specific, so that pattern is now written doctrine: denominator artifact, not a real move. When a dead tag made add-to-cart read 726%, the fix was a generic impossibility gate (more adders than viewers) that blanks the metric, shades the band, and self-heals when the backfill lands.

What changed

  • The hub is the operating cadence. Stakeholders open dashboards instead of requesting pulls.
  • Dashboards get retired when a better cut supersedes them. Curated, not accreted.
  • The Wednesday deck runs as one command: detect the fiscal week, run eleven modules against the mart, screenshot each view into deck-ready images. A mid-week correction is a re-run, not a rebuild.
  • A retail leader's question from a business review got its own permanently maintained tab.
Technical notes, for the people who will ask

Decomposition. Log-mean Divisia (LMDI-I) over the identity Revenue = Σtactic Clicks · share · (Sess/Click) · (Shop/Sess) · (Cart/Shop) · (CO/Cart) · (Orders/CO) · AOV: eight effects, additive, zero residual. The engine is a generic N-factor chain with unit tests, reused across three dashboards; the Python and client-side JS implementations are cross-checked on every generation.

Verified click lineage. The production bridge anchors paid clicks to sessions via click-ID matching (~85–90% coverage), so "clicks that never produced a measured visit" is a real, sized bar instead of a shrug.

A method rejected on sign. An earlier spend-mix valuation (Laspeyres-style, crediting added spend at last year's return) flipped the sign of a reallocation effect, because it valued new dollars at a rate measured on almost no spend. Replaced with an exact identity valuing dollars at their realized return.

Delivery. Single-file HTML with embedded data caches; daily-grain data so arbitrary ranges aggregate exactly; per-table CSV export that opens clean in Excel; scheduled refresh via ~36 OS-level jobs; the deck renderer drives headless Chrome for pixel-identical screenshots.
